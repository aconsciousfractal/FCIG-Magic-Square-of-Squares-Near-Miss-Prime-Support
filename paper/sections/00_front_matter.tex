\begin{abstract}
We study near misses to a $3\times3$ magic square of nine distinct positive
integer squares through the common difference of its transversal
progressions. Using the complete $S$-unit totals of von K\"anel and
Matschke, we reconstruct the corresponding square progressions and exclude
fully magic squares whose transversal difference has only prime factors
at most $19$. In the same domain we classify exactly $384$ primitive
nine-square arrays with seven equal line sums, up to grid symmetry and
common square scaling, and determine their sharp difference-normalized
defect. A separate consequence of Aebi's triangle classification gives
uniqueness of a positive square progression for every difference with at
most three prime factors, without a primitivity assumption.
For arrays with eight square entries, we construct two infinite families:
a retraction of classical six-square carriers with a complete least-shift
law, and an elliptic-orbit lift with an explicit positive index density.
We also classify a finite bridge of difference $840$.
For the eighth-line condition we distinguish a fixed-difference elliptic
interaction from a variable-difference model modulo scale; the latter is
an irreducible surface whose quintic reconstruction over the
square-progression cone has generic Galois group $S_5$. We specialize the finiteness
theorem of Caro and Garc\'ia-Fritz, close particular arithmetic fibres,
and derive local support restrictions. The general existence problem and
arbitrary four-prime supports remain open. Exact verifiers and identified
external computer-algebra records accompany the proofs.
\end{abstract}
