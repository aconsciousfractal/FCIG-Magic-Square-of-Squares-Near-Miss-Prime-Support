
\begin{abstract}
Whether a $3\times3$ magic square of nine distinct positive perfect squares
exists is a well-known open problem. This paper studies the nearest
structured objects along three axes. Call a $3\times3$ integer array an
\emph{$(8,7)$ array} if its nine entries are positive and pairwise distinct,
exactly eight of them are perfect squares, and exactly seven of its eight
ordinary lines (rows, columns, long diagonals) share one common sum; every
use of ``$(8,7)$ near miss'' below refers to this metric. We also classify
specified $(9,7)$ arrays, with all nine entries square. First, we
give a self-contained normal form --- every seven-line array is three
arithmetic progressions with one common difference --- together with the
integral Smith form of its line-sum map, and construct infinite $(8,7)$
families by three mechanisms: an antidiagonal retraction of the catalogued
Bremner--Wesolowski six-square carriers with a complete least-shift law, a
bridge completion in Fituvalu's type-6.VI frame, and an elliptic-orbit lift
on $\Esn$ giving a positive-density sequence of pairwise inequivalent
entry-primitive examples. Second, closing the eighth line is shown to be a rational-point
problem on one arithmetic surface: a single linear condition on
$\Esn^{3}$, whose saturated model is an irreducible surface carrying
an $S_5$-monodromy cone map; one fibre of the interaction is closed
completely, fixed-squareclass finiteness follows from an imported theorem
of Caro and Garc\'ia-Fritz, and the route through finite Kummer classes
alone is provably insufficient. Third, an $S$-unit law for the common
difference $d$ of the three progressions bounds the number of
configurations for every fixed prime support and excludes whole support
classes. A consequence of Aebi's classification gives at most one positive
square progression for any difference with at most three prime factors.
Using the complete $S$-unit totals of von K\"anel--Matschke, an exact
catalogue excludes fully magic squares whose transversal difference has
only primes at most $19$, and classifies the $384$ associated primitive
$(9,7)$ classes. Arbitrary four-prime supports remain open. The companion
repository supplies exact Python replays for the elementary and symbolic
computations, and hash-pinned inputs and normalized output records for the
external computer-algebra computations.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Setting and objects}

The following objects are kept distinct. An \emph{$(8,7)$ array} is
as in the abstract; a \emph{$(9,7)$ array} has nine distinct positive square
entries and exactly seven equal ordinary line sums. A \emph{six-square carrier} is a fully magic $3\times3$
array of six squares and three non-squares from the catalogued classical
families. A \emph{Parker configuration} is a triple of pairwise-distinct
three-term progressions of perfect squares with one \emph{common}
difference $d$, the structure forced on the three transversals of a carrier
by the completion problem. In the finiteness and support-law statements,
the configuration additionally satisfies the full-magic interaction
$s_1^2+s_2^2=2s_0^2$, with the positive, distinct-entry open locus imposed.
A general $(9,7)$ array does not satisfy this interaction. The support
statements concern $\supp(2d)$ ---
the prime support of the common difference --- and never the prime factors
of the entries of a hypothetical completed square, which is the object
studied by Rabern, Woll, Labruna and Weisenberg. The label recalls Matt
Parker's widely known Parker Square~\cite{PARKER}; it does not attribute the
underlying problem to Parker, and Parker's particular near miss is not a
configuration studied in this paper.

\section*{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[Seven-line normal form]\label{main:normalform}
Up to the dihedral symmetry of the grid, every $3\times3$ integer array in
which exactly seven of the eight ordinary lines share a common sum has the
antidiagonal as its failed line and is exactly
\[
\begin{pmatrix} A & P+D & E-D\\ P-D & E & A+D\\ E+D & A-D & P\end{pmatrix},
\]
three arithmetic progressions of one common difference $D$; the seven lines
sum to $A+E+P$ and the failed diagonal to $3E$. Consequently an $(8,7)$
array consists of two complete square progressions and one progression
containing exactly two squares. The integral line-sum map
$\Lambda:\mathbb Z^9\to\mathbb Z^8$ has Smith form
$\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,1,1,3,0)$; hence its cokernel is
$\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ and characteristic $3$ is the unique
prime at which its rank drops.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Infinite $(8,7)$ families]\label{main:families}
\emph{(i)} Subtracting $6z_n^2$ from the antidiagonal of the $n$-th
catalogued Bremner--Wesolowski carrier yields, for every $n\ge1$, an
$(8,7)$ array with explicit square entries and defect $12z_n^2$; every
odd-indexed member is root-primitive. \emph{(ii)} For fixed $n$, all
positive shifts $0<t<C$ closing at least one antidiagonal pair to squares
are classified by same-parity factor pairs of $\Delta$ and $2\Delta$, and the
least such shift is $9$ for $n=1$ and $6z_n^2$ for every $n\ge2$; it is not
unique. \emph{(iii)} In the type-6.VI frame the fixed bridge
$(23^2,37^2,47^2)$ of difference $840$ admits exactly the tautological
completion $(23,37,47)$ and one further completion $(79,65,89)$; coupled
with Robertson's progression $(1^2,29^2,41^2)$ it produces an explicit
$(8,7)$ array. \emph{(iv)} The non-torsion orbit of $P=(841,-1189)$ on
$\Esn\colon Y^2=X^3-840^2X$ re-enters the admissible window infinitely
often and yields infinitely many pairwise square-scale/$D_4$-inequivalent,
entry-primitive $(8,7)$ arrays from Bremner's Configuration VI. More
precisely, the admissible indices have natural density
\[
\delta=1-I_{(840/4225)^2}\!\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac12\right)
=0.6585271498271213\ldots .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The interaction surface]\label{main:surface}
\emph{(i)} After independent common subtractions $\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2$ on
the three difference-$840$ transversals of the fixed carrier, all rows and
columns remain equal and the two diagonals agree if and only if
$\tau_1+\tau_2=2\tau_0$; equivalently $x_1+x_2=2x_0$ with each $x_i$ in
$x\bigl(2\Esn(\Q)\bigr)$ --- one equation on $\Esn^{3}$. \emph{(ii)}
Saturating the associated polynomial system by every zero, denominator and
collision divisor leaves one irreducible surface of dimension two,
which maps onto the rational cone $2A^2=B^2+C^2$ with generic degree five
in squared coordinates (degree forty on the full signed cover) and generic
monodromy $S_5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finiteness and one closed fibre]\label{main:finiteness}
\emph{(i)} \textbf{Derived from an imported theorem} (Caro--Garc\'ia-Fritz,
Thms.~1.2--1.3): for fixed $d$, the points of $(2E_d(\Q))^{3}$ satisfying
the interaction, after removing points at infinity, nonpositive and
repeated $x$-coordinates, form a finite set, with the imported bound of shape
$D^{3r+1}$, $r=\operatorname{rank}E_d(\Q)$; hence for every fixed
squareclass of $d$ only finitely many positive distinct Parker
configurations exist modulo rational-square scaling. The bound is
non-effective. \emph{(ii)} \textbf{Computer-assisted} (the decisive rank
and torsion enter as a hash-verified frozen transcript of the official
Magma Calculator): the fibre $x_0=841$ is closed completely --- its
necessary genus-two curve has a complementary bielliptic quotient of rank
zero and full two-torsion, whose four rational points pull back to
$t\in\{0,\pm1,\pm841,\pm1681\}$, and positivity leaves only $t=0$, so
the fibre contains no positive nonconstant three-transversal completion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The finite-class stop]\label{main:kummerstop}
Half classes $\lambda_n(P)=[Q]\in E_d(\Q)/(2^nE_d(\Q)+E_d(\Q)[2])$ are well
defined for $P\in2E_d(\Q)$. On $\Esn$, neither $P\mapsto x(P)$ nor the
interaction of Theorem~\ref{main:surface} factors through the finite class
triple for any $n\ge1$: the explicit pair $Q$, $Q+2^nR$ has equal classes
and unequal doubled $x$-coordinates. Hence no obstruction retaining only
the three individual finite Kummer/2-Selmer classes can characterize the
interaction. The certified true witness is the repeated triple $(Q,Q,Q)$;
a stronger statement whose hypotheses first impose the positive
pairwise-distinct open locus is \emph{not} excluded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The support law]\label{main:supportlaw}
For a positive pairwise-distinct Parker configuration put $u_i=t_i-r_i$,
$v_i=t_i+r_i$, $z_i=u_i/v_i$. Then $u_iv_i=2d$, $0<z_i<1$, and the
interaction is exactly the five-term unit equation
\[
z_1+z_1^{-1}+z_2+z_2^{-1}=2\,(z_0+z_0^{-1}),
\]
nondegenerate on the Parker open. For every finite prime set
$\mathcal S\ni2$, the number of root-primitive configurations with
$\supp(2d)\subseteq\mathcal S$, modulo common rational-square scaling, is at
most
\[
\exp\bigl(30^{15}(3|\mathcal S|+1)\bigr).
\]
This counting theorem by itself produces no enumeration; the separate
fixed-support route is given in Section~\ref{sec:smoothsupport}. At every odd
prime the largest of $|v_p(z_i)|$ occurs at least twice; the admissible
endpoint pairs obey the explicit $(-1/p),(2/p)$ table; and
$\supp(2d)\subseteq\{2,3\}$ is impossible for a primitive configuration.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Complete three-prime exclusion]\label{main:threeprime}
\textbf{Consequence of Aebi's classification.} For any positive integer
$d$ with at most three distinct prime divisors, there is at most one
positive integer triple $r<s<t$ such that
$s^2-r^2=t^2-s^2=d$. No primitivity assumption is required. In particular,
neither a $(9,7)$ array nor a fully magic square of nine distinct positive
integer squares can have such a transversal difference. The primitive
Pythagorean-triangle classification is Aebi's; this statement is its
squareclass consequence, without a priority claim.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Smooth differences and a complete $(9,7)$ atlas]\label{main:smoothsupport}
\textbf{Computer-assisted, using imported complete $S$-unit totals.}
Let $S_B$ be the primes at most $B$. \emph{(i)} There is no fully magic
$3\times3$ square of nine distinct positive integer squares whose
transversal difference $d$ satisfies $\supp(d)\subseteq S_{19}$.
\emph{(ii)} The $(9,7)$ arrays with $\supp(|D|)\subseteq S_B$, modulo
$D_4$ and common rational-square scaling, have exactly $3,45,384$ classes
for $B=7,13,19$, respectively, represented by primitive positive roots.
\emph{(iii)} For $B=19$, the sharp lower bound for
$\eta=|A+P-2E|/|D|$ is $347984603/9837828000$, attained by exactly one
class, already present for $B=13$. These are unbounded-exponent statements:
completeness uses Theorem~A of von K\"anel--Matschke, not a search cutoff.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{The four-prime frontier}

For exact support $\supp(2d)=\{2,3,p,q\}$ the paper proves the complete
endpoint-incidence classification and edge identity $F_{10}=F_{02}$, the
balanced-branch reduction to seven fixed primitive cores, and the complete
exclusion of the outer double-balanced core-area-6 branch.
Aebi-core central genus-two fibres, outer fixed cores and persistent
unbalanced cores remain open beyond the finite-support exclusions of
Theorem~\ref{main:smoothsupport}; their residual states are in
Section~\ref{sec:frontier}.

\subsection*{What this paper does not claim}

No existence or nonexistence of a $3\times3$ magic square of nine distinct
positive squares in general; no global Parker impossibility; no effective
enumeration in an unrestricted squareclass; no arbitrary four-prime
exclusion or support-size bound; no exact ranks or
point lists beyond the certified ones; no decision of any object in the
companion dossiers (the residual cover $q_R(K)$, the $n\ge5$ lift tower, the
degree-90 class $[A_R]$ --- all open there); and \textbf{no novelty,
priority or firstness anywhere}: prior-art gates for such wording are
deliberately closed, and every no-hit search is reported as
``no antecedent located, firstness unproved''.
