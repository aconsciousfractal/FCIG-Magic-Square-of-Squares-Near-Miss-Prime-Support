\section{The least-shift interval analysis, and certificates}\label{app:certificates}

\subsection{The complete case analysis for Proposition~\ref{prop:leastshift}}

Setting and notation as in Section~\ref{sec:families}: $n\ge2$,
$(y,z)\mapsto(y+2z,y+3z)$ from $(11,15)$, induction inequalities
\[
z>y,\quad 2y>z,\quad 5y>3z,\quad 5z>6y,\quad 8y>5z,\quad z\ge15,
\]
$t_0=6z^2$, $h=t_0-t$ with $0<h<6z^2$, and at $t_0$ the three entries are
$C-t_0=Y^2$ ($Y=yz+1$), $E-t_0=H=4z^4-z^2+1$, $G-t_0=X^2$ ($X=xz-1$).

\emph{The $C$ entry.} If $C-t=(Y+a)^2$ then $h=a(2Y+a)$. If $a\ge5$ then
$h\ge5(2Y+5)=10yz+35>6z^2$ because $5y>3z$; hence only $a\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.

\emph{The $G$ entry.} If $G-t=(X+b)^2$ with $b\ge2$ then
$h\ge4X+4=4yz+8z^2>6z^2$ (here $X=xz-1$ and $x=y+2z$ along the
recurrence); thus $b=1$ and necessarily $h=2X+1=2yz+4z^2-1$.

\emph{The $E$ entry.} The exact neighbouring squares are
\[
(2z^2-1)^2<H<(2z^2)^2<(2z^2+1)^2<H+6z^2<(2z^2+2)^2,
\]
so $E-t$ can be square only for $h=z^2-1$ or $h=5z^2$.

\emph{Excluding $CE$.} Equating $a(2Y+a)$ with the two possible $E$
increments gives, for $a=1,2,3,4$ respectively:
\[
\begin{array}{r|c|c}
a & \text{equality with } z^2-1 & \text{equality with } 5z^2\\\hline
1 & z(z-2y)=4 & z(5z-2y)=3\\
2 & z(z-4y)=9 & z(5z-4y)=8\\
3 & z(z-6y)=16 & z(5z-6y)=15\\
4 & z(z-8y)=25 & z(5z-8y)=24
\end{array}
\]
The first column is negative in every row because $2y>z$. In the second
column, rows $1$ and $2$ equate positive multiples of $z\ge15$ with $3$ or
$8$; row $3$ has $5z-6y>0$, and for $z>15$ the product exceeds $15$ while
at $(y,z)=(11,15)$ it equals $135$; row $4$ is negative because $8y>5z$.
Branch $CE$ is impossible.

\emph{Excluding $CG$.} Equating the four $C$ increments with
$2yz+4z^2-1$ gives respectively
\[
4z^2=4,\qquad 2z(2z-y)=9,\qquad z(z-y)=4,\qquad 2z(2z-3y)=25 .
\]
The first and third contradict $z\ge15$; the second and fourth equate an
even integer with an odd one. Branch $CG$ is impossible.

\emph{Excluding $EG$.} The $G$ increment exceeds $z^2-1$, their difference
being $2yz+3z^2>0$; equality with $5z^2$ would give $z(2y-z)=1$,
contradicting $z\ge15$ and $2y>z$. Branch $EG$ is impossible.

Every pair is excluded, so no positive pair-closing shift is smaller than
$6z^2$; the shift $6z^2$ itself closes $C$ and $G$, and
Theorem~\ref{main:families}(i) shows it is nondegenerate. The proof is
uniform in $n$, uses no factorization of a particular $\Delta$ and no scan
over $t$; the factor-pair census for $2\le n\le20$ is corroboration only.
The statement deliberately starts at $n=2$: at $n=1$ the shift $9$ is
smaller than the uniform $6z^2=96$.

\subsection{Certificate discipline}

Every theorem of this paper is backed by an exact machine-checkable
certificate in the companion repository, consumed by a single verifier
entry point; the verifier re-derives the exact witnesses (the recurrence
identities and least shifts; the bridge divisor check and the $(79,65,89)$
completion; the elliptic-orbit witnesses with denominator clearing; the
saturation and monodromy data; the fibre algebra and pulled-back $t$-lists;
the half-class countercertificate; the torus, endpoint-table and
three-block identities; the four-prime incidence and fixed-fibre
identities) using exact integer and symbolic arithmetic only. Computations
performed on commercial computer algebra (the rank, torsion and complete
point-list gates attributed in the text to the official Magma Calculator
V2.29-8) enter exclusively as \emph{frozen transcripts} verified by hash;
the verifier checks the hashes and the parsed assertions, and never
requires that software to run. The exact file-level certificate map is
generated together with the verifier and shipped in the repository
manifest.
